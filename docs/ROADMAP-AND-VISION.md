## FUTURE VISION — product challenge: a new person's empty continuity core

The first complete portable Wildline experience should show: independent setup; authorized source ingestion; user-owned correction; visible uncertainty; fresh-session context recovery; and bounded recovery after failure. A teacher, leader or caregiver should develop their own working history rather than inherit the founder's preferences or identity.

This experience remains a goal. The separate Continuity Reader and this bounded public Bee Bridge derivative do not constitute that whole product.

## FUTURE VISION — research directions, not shipped features

### Wonder and governed challenge

We want an AI that recognizes patterns without deciding they are permanent. The 411/413/412 rhythm asks the system and user to recover, challenge and reconstruct an understanding rather than merely reinforce familiarity.

### The In-Between

We are exploring whether a continuing, governed human-AI working context makes new questions or options available, and how to test usefulness against simpler memory systems and ordinary conversation. This is an open research question, not a proven autonomous intelligence.

### Recognizable change, without a fixed identity

The original narrative draws inspiration from the Lorenz attractor and fractal geometry: a recognizable pattern may persist through changing trajectories. This is an **analogy**, not a claim that Wildline implements those mathematical systems or that human-AI collaboration follows proven fractal dynamics. It motivates a falsifiable design question: how can continuity remain recognizable without limiting future possibility?

### Person-governed living legacy

One possibility is continuity that helps a person revisit changing meaning across a lifetime and choose what to preserve. It must never pretend to replace them, record everything by default, or decide what their life meant.

### Caregiving and peer hospice support

A particularly meaningful long-term possibility is continuity to support caregivers, including peer hospice support involving incarcerated individuals. Any such application would need stringent privacy, consent, dignity, clinical/professional oversight, and institutional governance. There is **no deployed medical or correctional care product here**.
